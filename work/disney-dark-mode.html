Disney Parks – Dark Mode

Exploring a dark theme for Disney's parks app, built into the Vista design system so every screen and component can switch with its tokens.

Designed for
The Walt Disney Company
Date
2026
Role
Senior Product Designer
Result
Design system exploration

The problem

Guests lean on the parks app all day, often where a bright white screen works against them: watching fireworks and nighttime shows, waiting in dark ride queues, inside attractions, and late in a long day when the battery is running low. The app had no dark theme.

Why dark mode

  • Save battery. Dark surfaces draw less power on OLED phones over a full park day.
  • Easier viewing at night. A dimmer screen is easier on the eyes in a dark park and doesn't light up the people around you.
  • A dark variation for indoor spaces and queue lines for dark rides, where a bright screen is a distraction.

The approach

I built dark mode as a theme inside the Vista UI kit rather than as one-off screens, so components switch through design tokens. I audited every component side by side in light and dark, sorted them into Approved and Needs revisions, then tested the theme on real parks app screens: trip home, the trip checklist, Explore, account settings, parking vouchers and resort details.

Concerns and open questions

  • The "inverse" token does two jobs. In light mode, inverse text is white, which happens to be right both for text on fixed brand colors (buttons, destructive actions, map pins, anything over a photo) and for text on true inverse surfaces like tooltips and snackbars. In dark mode they need to split: text on fixed colors stays white, while inverse surfaces should flip.
  • All 128 component bindings to inverse turned out to be the fixed-color case. I proposed renaming them to "on-color" and adding a true inverse set. That's a breaking token-name change for engineering, so it was tried and rolled back in October 2026 while the team weighs the cost.
  • Fixed brand fills can't get lighter than the point where white text still clears 4.5:1 contrast.
  • Warning toasts use white text while the others use color-specific text, which needs a closer look.
  • Disabled states are moving to percentage-based fills.
  • Map pins and floating action menus need higher-contrast hover states on a dark map.

Trip home, light and dark

The same screen in both themes. Hero imagery keeps its full color; surfaces, cards and type shift to the dark palette, while fixed brand colors like the red toast and Cancel My Trip button keep white text.

Explore, a first pass

Dropping a dark theme onto existing screens exposed problems: section headings and card copy that stayed dark on a dark background, and text over images that lost contrast. Each one went on the list of components to fix.

Trip checklist

A dense, task-heavy screen. In dark mode, cards separate from the background with lighter surfaces instead of shadows, which disappear on dark backgrounds.

Parks app scope

The screens in scope for the exploration, each shown in light and dark: trip home, the trip checklist, Explore, account and settings, parking vouchers and resort details.

Component audit

Every component in the Vista UI kit compared side by side in light and dark, then sorted into Approved and Needs revisions.

Toast messaging

Status colors become deep tinted fills, so info, success, warning and error stay distinct without glowing in a dark room. Warning uses white text while the others use color-specific text, which is flagged for review.

Floating action menu

Interactive states were rebuilt for dark: hover and pressed get lighter instead of darker, and the selected state moves to a tinted surface.

Map pins

Pins need to stay easy to find on a dark map. Hover and pressed states flip to lighter fills, and both pins and floating action menus were flagged for higher-contrast hover states.

Large cards across themes

The same attraction card in Parks light, Cruise light and dark. One set of tokens drives each brand and theme.
