Disney Parks – Dark Mode

Exploring a dark theme for Disney's parks app, built into the Vista design system so every screen and component can switch with its tokens.

The Walt Disney CompanyDesigned for
2026Date
Senior Product DesignerRole
Design system explorationResult

The problem

Guests use the parks app all day, often where a bright white screen gets in the way: at nighttime shows, in dark ride queues and late in the day on a low battery. The app had no dark theme.

Why dark mode

  • Saves battery
  • Easier viewing at night
  • Less glare in dark rides and indoor queues

The approach

I built dark mode into the Vista design system so every component switches with tokens. Each component was reviewed side by side in light and dark, then the theme was tested on real parks app screens.

Open questions

  • One "inverse" token does two jobs that need to split in dark mode. Renaming it is a breaking change, so it is on hold while the team weighs the cost.
  • Brand colors can only get so light before white text loses contrast.
  • Toasts, disabled states, map pins and menus still need contrast tuning.

Every component, in both themes

Dark mode had to work on every single component, not just whole screens. We ran each one through both themes side by side: calendars, date pickers, dropdowns, buttons, search, toggles, checkboxes and segmented controls. For each, we checked that it still read clearly, kept its contrast and behaved the same way, so a selected date or an active toggle is just as obvious at night as it is in daylight.

Finding the right background

A straight dark theme gets very dark, very fast, and loses the lightness and brightness people expect from Disney. To bring the whimsy and magic back, we tried a range of backgrounds on the same Trip Plans screen, from flat navy to soft gradients with hints of blue and purple. The gradients kept the screen easy on the eyes at night while still feeling like a Disney sky.

Trip checklist

A dense, task-heavy screen. In dark mode, cards separate from the background with lighter surfaces instead of shadows, which disappear on dark backgrounds.

Alert components

Toasts and badges, light against dark. Status colors become deep tinted fills, so success, warning, error and info stay easy to tell apart without glowing in a dark room, and every badge style keeps its meaning in both themes.
